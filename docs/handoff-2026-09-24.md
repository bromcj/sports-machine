## The results, in one place

**A1 — MLB moneyline, market-anchored.** b1 = +0.103, CI [−0.073, +0.273],
t = +1.20: the model's disagreement with the price is indistinguishable from
noise. The anchored model does not beat the 10 a.m. price it is anchored to.

The movement test was **not** as predicted and is the one interesting result in
the whole task: the model *does* predict where the market moves between 10 a.m.
and the close, **+0.0241, t = +6.5**, positive in all three seasons and
surviving removal of the starter features. It is worth **0.17 probability
points** against a ~2-point break-even — about **twelve times too small**.
Betting it directly: 633 bets, 34.3% won against a 37.5% break-even, **−9.5%**.

**Part E — six hypotheses, zero passed.**

- **E1** (the central one: market overvalues starters, undervalues bullpens).
  Built proper measures first — Marcel-style starter projection with
  xwOBA-against and velocity, leverage-weighted bullpen from Statcast
  `delta_home_win_exp`, fatigue from the top three arms. Decisive residual
  regression: best term **t = −1.31**, three of five terms wrong-signed. The
  market weights starter quality +0.111 against reality's +0.122 (t = −0.27)
  and bullpen quality +0.069 against +0.051 (t = +0.64). **The market's recipe
  matches reality's.**
- **E2** no bucket clears t = 2 in the predicted direction.
- **E3** no public subgroup where the morning price beats the close — every
  subgroup favours the close, and big-market games favour it *more*.
- **E4** all three directions wrong. Popular teams are priced *lower* than
  Pinnacle, not higher (t = −7.4 to −9.5), no weekend effect, no time pattern.
- **E5** heavy favourites +2.73 points at Pinnacle, **t = +1.46**. Right
  direction, underpowered.
- **E6** April–May +0.0028 against July–August +0.0024 — identical. Velocity
  wrong-signed early.

## Things the next brief should probably take as settled

1. **Public-data models do not beat main markets here.** MLB moneylines, NFL
   sides and NFL receiving props have all now been measured against real
   de-vigged prices and all lost. Another model of the same shape, on the same
   inputs, is not worth buying data for.
2. **There is almost nothing to shop between our books.** Betting every NJ
   price against Pinnacle returns −3.8% to −4.4%; inside 90 minutes of first
   pitch a price worth >1.5% EV appears about **once in 104 games** across
   DraftKings, FanDuel and BetMGM combined. This is a *lower bound* — we hold
   four books, a real shop watches twelve — and it is the main reason Part C
   was not built.
3. **Props are not the soft market the brief assumed on price.** Vig is
   5.6–7.1% against 3.9–4.6% on moneylines. Break-even is near **53%**. "Softer"
   is true of the *line*, not of the *price*.
4. **Pinnacle DOES price NFL props** — live and back to 2023. The brief
   expected otherwise. But `regions=us` **excludes** Pinnacle (it is not
   US-licensed), so it must be named in `bookmakers=`. A named list counts as
   one region however long: **nine books for one credit.**
5. **Confirmation seasons were not bought, deliberately.** Nothing passed
   screening, and the near-misses would not cross t = 2 even with 2022–23
   added (E1 1.31 → 1.67, E5 1.46 → 1.88). ~28,000 credits for three results
   that still say "no edge found".

## Methodological notes worth carrying into the next brief

These are the things that nearly produced false findings. All three were caught,
and a brief that wants trustworthy answers should keep asking for this.

- **A1's movement slope.** `logit(p_morning)` sat on both sides of the
  regression, so measurement error in one snapshot manufactures a positive
  slope. The obvious fix — swapping one measurement for the other — is itself
  biased, *downward*, by the same mechanism; the two naive versions straddle
  the truth (+0.0284 and +0.0096, truth +0.0241). The unbiased estimator uses
  both measurements in both places.
- **E5's favourite–longshot bias.** Selecting "heavy favourites" with each
  book's own de-vigged price selects *different games* at each book, because
  de-vigging bends favourites by an amount that scales with the margin.
  Pinnacle found 536, BetMGM 462, and BetMGM's were stronger. That alone
  produced a clean-looking gradient from +2.73 to +4.97 across books. With the
  sample fixed once, it collapses to +2.73…+3.12 — the books agree.
- **B3's pre-registered feature was a column of zeros.** `vacated_share` joined
  the injury report onto the weekly player table — but a player ruled out *has
  no row* in that week's stats, because nflverse only records men who played.
  The subgroup test ran with one group in it.

Two others worth naming: the props model was **wildly over-confident** (one
pooled `Var/mean` for a negative binomial, when that ratio grows with the mean),
and it was given a deliberately generous second scoring — walk-forward Platt
scaling — so that "no signal" could not be confused with "bad calibration". It
still lost by 11 SE.
